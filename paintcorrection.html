Why It Matters

What Paint Correction Actually Does

Every wash, every winter, every automated brush adds up. Swirl marks and fine scratches live in the clear coat — the thin protective layer over your paint. A wax or sealant sits on top of them; it can't remove them. Paint correction does. Machine polishing levels the clear coat so light reflects evenly again, and the color underneath looks deeper and wetter than it has in years.

Signs your vehicle needs correction

  • Swirl marks or spider webbing visible in sunlight
  • Fine scratches from washing, drying, or brush tunnels
  • Dull or chalky paint, especially on the roof and hood
  • Water spots that won't wash off
  • Paint that looks "flat" even right after a wash

What correction won't fix

  • Scratches deep enough to catch a fingernail
  • Rock chips, rust, or peeling clear coat
  • Dents or body damage

If we find damage that needs body shop work, we'll tell you before starting — not after.

Our Process

How a Paint Correction Goes

1. Inspection

We look at your clear coat under proper lighting and measure paint thickness where needed. Then we confirm the price and scope before any work starts.

2. Wash & Decontamination

Before any polishing, the paint is washed and clay-bar treated so we're not grinding dirt into the clear coat. This step is non-negotiable.

3. Machine Polishing

We run multiple machine passes with the appropriate pad and compound. Each pass is checked under light before moving on.

4. Protection

Once the finish is where it should be, we apply a wax or sealant to protect the corrected clear coat and make maintenance easier going forward.
